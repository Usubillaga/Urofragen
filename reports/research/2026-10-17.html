1. LUNAR: longer follow-up, still no change in standard care

Relevance grade: 2 — relevant. It directly informs the distinction between radioligand study options and the treatment settings taught in both projects.

Original title: 177Lu-Prostate-specific Membrane Antigen Prior to Stereotactic Ablative Radiotherapy in Oligorecurrent Prostate Cancer: Secondary and Exploratory End Point Results from the Randomized Phase 2 LUNAR Trial. Amar U. Kishan et al., UCLA; European Urology. Online first 12 October 2026: a new secondary/exploratory update to an already reported RCT, rather than a new primary trial. Original full text, DOI 10.1016/j.eururo.2026.09.005.

[Sicher] In hormone-sensitive oligorecurrent prostate cancer with 1–5 PSMA-PET lesions, 92 patients were randomized to SBRT alone or two preceding cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 plus SBRT. Five controls withdrew immediately; the reported analysis includes 87. At median follow-up of 31.9 months, median PFS was 17.6 versus 7.4 months, HR 0.38 (95% CI 0.23–0.61); hormonal therapy-free survival: HR 0.57 (0.34–0.95). Longer follow-up and additional endpoints are new. No new safety signals were reported; detailed adverse-event rates in the supplement were not checked here.

Limitations: single center, post-randomization attrition, no blinded central imaging review, PSMA-PET-driven PFS. Exploratory endpoints generate hypotheses; the 95% CI for CT-PFS includes 1. No established overall-survival benefit.

Project consequence [Wahrscheinlich]: source/explanation review. Read: uro-ope-00023 v1, CME-Salvage question 3 and bank-uro-ope-00023 in data/clinic.json: pelvic ENRT/boost/ADT for nodal recurrence is a different comparison. LUNAR does not test that strategy. Conversely, uro-pca-00018 v3 and bank-uro-pca-00018 concern pretreated mCRPC; LUNAR does not invalidate their keys. Review question: are the SBRT study option, ENRT/ADT and licensed mCRPC radioligand therapy clearly separated?

Assessment: clinically relevant; randomized phase 2 with limited generalizability. Germany: current Pluvicto product information, section 4.1, specifies progressive PSMA-positive mCRPC after ARPI and taxane; it provides no authorization for the LUNAR population or PNT2002.

2. RCC with caval thrombus: neoadjuvant SABR as a research approach

Relevance grade: 2 — relevant. It directly relates to the existing caval-thrombus case and requires careful explanation of the research setting.

Original title: Neoadjuvant Stereotactic Ablative Radiotherapy Before Inferior Vena Cava Thrombectomy for Renal Cell Carcinoma: A Phase 2 Nonrandomized Clinical Trial. Raquibul Hannan et al., UT Southwestern; JAMA Oncology. Published online 15 October 2026. Original full text, DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4172.

[Sicher] This prospective single-arm study examined SABR before nephrectomy/caval thrombectomy in RCC with Mayo II–IV thrombus: 23 treated, 22 operated, 21 in the primary RFS analysis; three initially had metastatic disease. One-year RFS was 71.4% (95% CI 47.2–86.0) against a historical surgery benchmark of 55.7%. With prespecified screening alpha 0.20 and p 0.09, the phase 2 criterion was met, without establishing conventional randomized superiority. Median follow-up: 108.6 months.

Harms/limitations: 25 grade-3 and four grade-4 treatment-related events, rather than patient rates. Four grade-3 events were attributed to radiotherapy; no radiation-related grade-4/5 events and no 90-day mortality occurred. A small selected population, historical control and broad uncertainty prevent a causal efficacy conclusion. The 15.7-percentage-point difference is not an established treatment effect.

Project consequence [Wahrscheinlich]: source/explanation review. Read: uro-rcc-00012 v1 and imported bank-uro-rcc-00012 in data/theatre.json, concerning atrial thrombus and multidisciplinary surgical planning. Answer B/b is unaffected by this study. Review question: can the research option be explained without replacing necessary expert-team planning with irradiation? Mayo thrombus height is distinct from TNM; no classification relabeling.

Assessment: major clinical implications, low certainty of comparative benefit. Germany: this radiotherapy trial establishes no drug-authorization change; no new guideline recommendation follows from it.

3. EMA: Truqap prostate cancer application withdrawn

Relevance grade: 3 — somewhat relevant. It matters for future additions, but no matching drug entry was found in the current learning data.

Original source: EMA, Meeting highlights from the Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) 12-15 October 2026, published 16 October 2026. Official notice.

[Sicher] The application to extend capivasertib/Truqap to PTEN-deficient metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer was withdrawn. This is regulatory news, without a new randomized efficacy analysis. The notice supplies no reliable effect size or adverse-event rate for the application; withdrawal alone does not establish lack of efficacy. Current German product information, section 4.1 specifies combination with fulvestrant for appropriately pretreated ER-positive/HER2-negative advanced breast cancer with a PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alteration, without a prostate indication.

Project consequence: no change required to the answer keys read. Neither project's current source data contained a capivasertib/Truqap entry. Targeted question for future additions: is study activity being misrepresented as EU authorization? Clinical relevance: moderate; regulatory evidence: high; prostate authorization in Germany from this application: no.

Highest review priority [Wahrscheinlich]: clearly separate the three prostate treatment settings in item 1. This source report changes no learning content or approvals and grants no specialist signoff.
